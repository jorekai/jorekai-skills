# Link quality gate

A placement passes when all three hold:

1. **Real traffic.** The site ranks for something (WebSearch its name plus a topic; or a backlink index shows organic traffic above a few hundred visits a month). Pages nobody reads pass no signal and attract no clicks.
2. **Topical match.** The linking page is about your topic or an adjacent one. A boating page linking to a boating course is a signal; a crypto blog linking to it is noise, or a spam signal.
3. **Natural placement.** A reader arriving at that sentence would expect the link. Contextual, inside the body, with an anchor that describes the target. Sidebars, footers, and author bios count for little.

Drop on any of these, whatever the price:

- Link pages, "resources" pages with hundreds of outbound links, PBNs, or sites selling links openly.
- Sites where most outbound links are `sponsored` or paid.
- Anchor text demands that stuff the exact keyword into every link; vary anchors (brand, URL, query, natural phrase).
- Any placement that needs to hide the commercial relationship.
- Patterns Google's spam policy lists by name (checked 2026-09-02): excessive link exchanges and partner pages built for cross-linking, low-quality directory or bookmark links, keyword-rich links in distributed widgets, links in the footers or templates of many sites, forum comments or signatures with optimized links, advertorials and press releases with optimized anchors that pass ranking credit.
- A third-party section on a host site (coupon, casino, or "partner" content) that exists to borrow the host's ranking: site reputation abuse, enforced by manual action since May 2024.

Digital PR (a study, a tool, a data page that journalists cite) is the documented-safe form of link earning; Mueller (2021) called it "a shame it often gets bucketed with the spammy kind of link building". For a local business, Google's own prominence definition names links to the business and review count as inputs; local citations beyond that are a heuristic.
